Related-Work OutlineThe related-work section must transition the reader from the inadequacies of log-odds mapping to the necessity of significance-gated pseudo-counts, navigating the threats of data incest and bandwidth saturation. The narrative structure follows a six-paragraph progression.Paragraph 1: The Transition to Conjugate Volumetric Mapping.
Traditional occupancy mapping relies on log-odds formulations that collapse uncertainty into rigid binary states, limiting adaptability in dynamic environments. Recent advancements utilizing Beta-distributed conjugate pseudo-counts for occupancy and Dirichlet distributions for semantic mapping resolve this inflexibility. However, these additive conjugate priors introduce a severe communication penalty: continuous ray-carving increments pseudo-counts smoothly, creating a relentless stream of minor state changes that overwhelms naive "send-on-update" mechanisms during multi-robot synchronization.Paragraph 2: The Bandwidth Bottleneck in Collaborative SLAM.
Real-world multi-robot systems are fundamentally bottlenecked by map sharing over degraded communication links. Current deployed frameworks, such as DOOR-SLAM and Kimera-Multi, mitigate network saturation by exchanging only sparse artifacts—such as trajectory keyframes, bounding boxes, or degraded-resolution submaps. While effective for localization, these sparsification techniques force the fleet to sacrifice dense posterior fidelity, preventing rigorous decentralized Bayesian fusion on the volumetric map layer.Paragraph 3: Limitations of Discrete Change Detection.
Volumetric delta-transmission techniques currently dominate applied map sharing, yet they universally gate on discrete state boundaries. Mechanisms such as the resetChangeDetection in OctoMap or the overwrite grids in VDB_Mapping transmit data only when a voxel crosses the threshold between free and occupied. Similarly, underwater acoustic transmission strategies prioritize bounding boxes of discrete state flips to survive ultra-low bandwidths. These discrete gates starve receivers of vital confidence gradients, preventing the peer from distinguishing between a sparsely observed "probable obstacle" and a densely observed "certain obstacle."Paragraph 4: Learned Compression vs. Probabilistic Interpretability.
In response to bandwidth constraints, collaborative perception has increasingly shifted toward deep-learning-based feature sparsification. Architectures such as Where2comm and InfoCom achieve extreme kilobyte-scale compression by transmitting spatial confidence masks and encoded feature embeddings. While highly efficient, transmitting non-interpretable feature embeddings prevents multi-robot systems from maintaining mathematically sound probabilistic maps. This breaks downstream data association for safety-critical tasks that require explicit, mathematically guaranteed Bayesian uncertainty.Paragraph 5: Event-Triggered Estimation and Information Age.
Gating transmission on divergence from a last-transmitted reference is a known optimal strategy in control theory, formalized as send-on-delta or event-triggered estimation. In communication networks, the efficiency of this paradigm is quantified by the Age of Incorrect Information (AoII), which penalizes staleness only when the receiver's state divergence exceeds an error threshold. The proposed mean-arm trigger instantiates AoII semantics directly upon a conjugate Bayesian belief, adapting classical event-triggered deadbands to volumetric mapping.Paragraph 6: Navigating Decentralized Fusion and Data Incest.
Sharing additive pseudo-counts across a decentralized fleet risks severe overconfidence due to data incest, where shared observational ancestry is double-counted by the receiver. While mathematically rigorous pooling methods, such as Generalized Covariance Intersection (GCI) for Beta and Dirichlet distributions, exist to mitigate this, they impose high computational overhead. The proposed mechanism sidesteps this complexity: by transmitting absolute pseudo-counts and executing a snapshot-replace merge, the gate enforces conservative fusion, trading absolute convergence speed for protection against double-counting and bandwidth saturation.4. Threat Register and Vulnerability AnalysisThe following vulnerabilities pose severe risks to the manuscript's framing, claims, and likelihood of acceptance. They are ranked by severity, accompanied by required rebuttal strategies and architectural concessions.4.1 The Censored-Beta Problem (Conjugacy Break)Severity: Critical
The Threat: The proposed manuscript claims the transmission gate is "exact in the limit" and "does not invalidate downstream Bayesian fusion." Mathematically, this assertion is fragile. Applying a deterministic deadband (the $\tau$ threshold) breaks conjugacy. During the inter-transmission intervals, the receiver is implicitly informed that the sender's voxel belief has not deviated beyond $\tau$. Consequently, the receiver's true theoretical posterior is a censored or truncated Beta distribution, not a standard Beta distribution. This phenomenon is well-documented in event-triggered estimation literature addressing Tobit models and censored Gaussians. If the receiver continues to fuse independent local measurements assuming a standard Beta prior, the variance is systematically overestimated, and the mean is biased.
Rebuttal Strategy: The claim "exact in the limit" survives exclusively because the wire carries absolute pseudo-count values rather than incremental deltas. Upon a triggering event, the receiver's distorted posterior is entirely overwritten by the sender's true, uncensored Beta parameters. The manuscript must explicitly soften its claims: state that the receiver's belief is a mathematically bounded approximation during silence, but it recovers exact conjugacy upon a transmission. The text must cite Han et al. (2015) to formally concede the censored estimation problem, highlighting the absolute-replacement architecture as the necessary mitigating mechanism.4.2 Data Incest and Double Counting in Fleet TopologiesSeverity: High
The Threat: The substrate utilizes additive pseudo-counts. If multiple robots observe the same environment and transmit absolute counts to a central merger, a critical vulnerability arises regarding the merging operator. If the merger adds the counts ($n_{merge} = n_A + n_B$), it double-counts any shared observational history, leading to catastrophic overconfidence. Peer reviewers versed in decentralized fusion will immediately flag this violation of data independence, pointing to Generalized Covariance Intersection (GCI) or Chernoff fusion as the mandatory corrective measure.
Rebuttal Strategy: The manuscript must explicitly define the merging operator and defend its simplicity. If the architecture utilizes a strict star-topology, data incest is minimized but not eliminated. The manuscript must state that the snapshot-replace mechanism acts as a form of "conservative fusion" (e.g., taking the maximum pseudo-count rather than summing them) to prevent runaway overconfidence. Furthermore, the text should cite Gao et al. (2022), acknowledging that while GCI exists for Beta-distributed variables, the bandwidth gate presented is orthogonal to the chosen fusion operator. The contribution is the synchronization trigger, not the decentralized fusion algebra.4.3 Re-invention of Age of Incorrect Information (AoII)Severity: Moderate
The Threat: The manuscript measures "peer lag"—defined as the time required for a voxel's merged value to come within $\epsilon$ of the sender's true value. This conceptualization is functionally identical to the Age of Incorrect Information (AoII), formalized by Maatouk et al. (2020). Claiming "peer lag" as a novel evaluation metric will draw immediate hostility from networking and communications reviewers, framing the paper as ignorant of foundational status-update theory.
Rebuttal Strategy: The manuscript must embrace the existing literature. The custom metric must be renamed to "Belief AoII" or "Thresholded AoII." Framing the evaluation as a novel application of AoII to robotic map synchronization adds cross-disciplinary rigor, preemptively neutralizing the reinvention critique and aligning the work with established telecommunications theory.4.4 Underwater Acoustic Prior Art (Discrete vs. Continuous)Severity: Moderate
The Threat: Systems designed for extreme bandwidth hostility, such as VDB_Mapping and underwater acoustic map transmission by Łuczyński et al. (2017), already gate volumetric map transmission based on last-transmitted states. The existence of aggressive per-cell gates in the literature threatens the novelty of the core mechanism.
Rebuttal Strategy: The defense relies entirely on the distinction between discrete and continuous states. Previous systems trigger only when a voxel crosses a binarized threshold (e.g., from "unknown" to "free"). The proposed mechanism gates on the gradients of continuous confidence. The manuscript's secondary claim—the evidence arm, which fires on confidence growth at a constant probability to prevent starvation of saturated voxels—is the crucial differentiator. The evidence arm allows a peer to learn the difference between "probably a wall" and "certainly a wall," a capability completely absent in discrete acoustic transmission systems. This must be heavily emphasized in the introduction and evaluation.4.5 Patent Landscape Verification (Intel CPS)Severity: Low
The Threat: Initial survey flagged Intel patents US 12,236,779 B2 and US 2023/0110467 A1 as potential prior art regarding differential cost-map transmission.
Rebuttal Strategy: Verification confirms these filings cover generic Collective Perception Service (CPS) architectures, 2D occupancy grid generation, and fractional road usage containers (RUMC). They do not disclose or claim KL-divergence-based deadbands applied to Beta/Dirichlet pseudo-counts. No architectural changes to the paper are required. However, to prevent automated prior-art matching during the review process, the manuscript should avoid utilizing the specific phrase "collective perception service."5. The following objections represent the sharpest probable attacks on the manuscript's framing, methodology, and evaluation.Objection 1: The "Exact in the Limit" Fallacy (Framing)Critique: "The authors claim their mechanism is 'exact in the limit' and 'does not invalidate downstream Bayesian fusion.' This is theoretically flawed. Applying a deterministic deadband creates a censored distribution. Because the receiver's fusion engine does not account for this censored Beta distribution during silence, the variance is systematically overestimated. It is not an exact Bayesian posterior."
Rebuttal Strategy: Concede and clarify. Acknowledge Han et al. (2015) regarding the mathematics of censored posteriors. Clarify that the claim of "exactness" applies strictly to the convergence state upon receipt of the absolute update, which overrides local distortion. The text must be softened to state that the receiver holds a bounded approximation during silence, but uniquely avoids the permanent, unrecoverable drift inherent to incremental delta transmission.Objection 2: Unresolved Data Incest (System Architecture)Critique: "The proposed system assumes a fleet maintaining additive pseudo-counts. If multiple robots traverse the same environment, their overlapping observations will be sent as absolute counts to the merger. If the merger adds them, it double-counts evidence. Rebuttal Strategy: Defend via strict isolation of scope. State that the map synchronization protocol is agnostic to the specific conflict-resolution strategy of the merger. Cite Generalized Covariance Intersection (GCI) for Dirichlet distributions as the mathematically correct method to merge absolute counts. Explicitly define the scope of the paper: the contribution is the synchronization trigger that governs network traffic, not the resolution of the decentralized fusion algebra itself.Objection 3: Redundancy of the Evidence Arm (Novelty)Critique: "The 'evidence arm' fires when confidence grows but probability remains constant. By doing so, the receiving robot loses the Bayesian uncertainty gradient entirely. Rebuttal Strategy: This objection cannot be beaten on bandwidth metrics; it must be conceded and reframed. InfoCom achieves kilobyte-scale bandwidth, which pseudo-count transmission cannot match. Compare strictly against OctoMap and VDB_Mapping, and explicitly state that learned feature compression is out of scope due to its inherent destruction of the explicit Bayesian posterior.
